## Neither Artificial nor Intelligent

> "AI is **neither artificial nor intelligent**. Rather, artificial intelligence is both embodied and material, made from natural resources, fuel, human labor, infrastructures, logistics, histories, and classifications. AI systems are not autonomous, rational, or able to discern anything without extensive, computationally intensive training with large datasets or predefined rules and rewards."

> "At a fundamental level, AI is **technical and social practices, institutions and infrastructures, politics and culture**. Computational reason and embodied work are deeply interlinked: AI systems both reflect and produce social relations and understandings of the world."

Crawford, Kate. (2021). *Atlas of AI* (p. 8). Yale University Press.

## Three Eras of Modernity

**17th / 18th Centuries — Dawn of Modernity**
 - Renaissance → Enlightenment Rationality
 - Mathematics (Descartes, Leibniz, Newton)
 - Science (Bacon, Newton); Philosophy (Descartes, Spinoza, Leibniz)
 - The Birth of the (political) Subject (Luther, Calvin → Descartes, Rousseau)
 - "Calculemus!" (Let us Calculate — Leibniz)
 - Proto-Industrialization / Globalization / Colonization
 - Machines: Steam Engine / Jacquard Loom

**19th / early 20th — Modernity Proper**
 - England as the Factory of the World: High precision mass mechanization
 - The Horror of the machine — Romanticism, Frankenstein, the Gothic genre
 - 1820s: Luddism, English Socialism
 - 1830s/40s: Babbage, Lovelace, Engels, Marx
 - Comte: Positivist sociology; Darwin: Man is not the centre of things
 - Ricoeur: "Hermeneutics of Suspicion" (Marx, Nietzsche, Freud)
 - Fordism / Taylorism

**mid 20th – early 21st — Post/Late Modernity**
 - Politics and economics: false "sciences" (2 world wars, Great Depression)
 - Computation arises from negative mathematics, 1930s (Gödel, Church, Turing): what computation **cannot** do
 - Post-war 1950s: AI, cybernetics, information theory (Shannon), the Turing test
 - Markets as perfect computers: neoliberalism as epistemology
 - Counter-narratives: Cultural Marxism, existentialism, poststructuralism
 - AI Winter / Spring / glorious Summer?
 - Transhumanism, return to Enlightenment, or enshittification (Doctorow)?

## Looking ahead...

What **fundamentals** make for AI / Machine Learning / Deep Learning / Neural networks?

 - Calculus (17th century — Leibniz, Newton)
 - Probability (18th/19th century — Euler, Gauss, Laplace, Fourier)
 - Linear algebra (19th century — James Joseph Sylvester)
 - Markov Models (very early 20th century — Andrey Markov)

## And a bold proposition...

 - All the **mathematics** for AI in 2025 was developed by the end of the 19th century (with applications, like Markov models, in 1906/1913)
 - Are the last 125 years just **hardware**, **networks** & **data**? (see LeCun 2021)
 - Is our sense of **modernity** just the long shadow cast by the Enlightenment (17th / 18th century)?
