WHY THIS COURSE EXISTS

In 2026, AI is no longer novel. The challenge is depth, nuance, multiple perspectives. This course contests simple narratives by introducing six lenses. By semester's end, you hold several valid, sometimes contradictory views simultaneously.

The Six Lenses

One per week. Each lens highlights different aspects of AI's role in education.

Six Weekly Themes

Historical: Where did AI come from? What paths led here?
Technological: How do these systems actually work?
Practitioner: How are educators using AI now?
Critical: What problems persist? What costs?
Pedagogical: How should we teach with and about AI?
Futurological: What futures are we building toward?

Education Issues: Cons

Plagiarism and cognitive offloading

Biased results and unequal access

The Problem

Deskilling: expertise transfers to machines

Perverse incentives: automated assignments → automated grading → hollow degrees

Deeper Issue

Bernard Stiegler asked: What happens when the automatic society becomes fully automated?

If machines handle assignment creation, grading, feedback, curriculum design—where does human judgment live?

Not just technology. This is about what education is — the role of adult attention and care in human development.

Education Issues: Pros

IF DESIGNED WELL

Potential Strengths

But: expensive, time-consuming, requires deep pedagogical thought.

1Personalized learning — paths adapt to student needs
2Accessibility — content remixed for different styles
3Universal Design for Learning (UDL) — inclusive by architecture
CAN AI GENERATE MULTIPLE FORMS OF ENGAGEMENT, REPRESENTATION, ACTIVITY?

Or does it flatten toward statistical average?

The central pedagogical question. Personalization is easy at scale; meaningful differentiation is not.

Three Structural Threats

Beyond education-specific issues. Systemic risks.
ERASURE OF COGNITIVE WORK

Programming, design, games, entry-level professions — the knowledge economy jobs.
What replaces them?

WHEN AI WORKS PROPERLY

Social media was harbinger: addictive, compulsive, psychologically corrosive.
What happens when an AI tutor is too effective, too available, too attuned to your weaknesses?

CONCENTRATION OF POWER

Who owns the AI? Who can afford advanced versions? The rest of us?
Inequality baked in at infrastructure level.

What to Learn? What to Teach?

The central pedagogical question: given AI's trajectory, what knowledge, skills, dispositions matter now?
NOT VIABLE AT SCALE

Specialized Technical Paths

These demand mathematical sophistication most students lack.

1Machine learning theory (requires calculus, probability, linear algebra)
2Hardware engineering (specialized; traditionally male-dominated)
3"Prompt engineering" (but AI can write prompts)
POTENTIALLY VIABLE

Service & Care Sectors

1Hands-on work: plumbing, electrical (Jensen Huang's suggestion)
2Care industries: hospitals, childcare, counseling (human presence, judgment, dignity)
3Humanities: creative practice, ethical reasoning, storytelling (what Thiel called the "unmeasurable")
NO EASY ANSWERS

We teach for futures we cannot predict in fields that may be obsolete at graduation.

Crisis in Higher Education

Spiraling costs — tuition unsustainable
Loss of institutional trust — COVID, misinformation, social fragmentation
Broken pathways — degrees no longer guarantee professional mobility or income
Anticipated disruption — once AI credibly replaces degree value
Higher ed is already stressed before AI arrives. AI's disruption hits a system in crisis. Not future threat; conditions present now.
RESEARCH EXPERIMENT OVER WINTER

Case Study: Geist in the Machine

THE PAPER

Research in Progress

Geist in the Machine: Simulating Recognition and Inner Dialogue in AI-Mediated Teaching and Research

https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10450

Extends The Drama Machine (character development in LLM agents)

Vibe coding (Karpathy): iterative refinement of system "personality"
Recognition & self-consciousness (Hegel 1807; Honneth 1996): we become aware through others' recognition
The unconscious (Freud 1923): drives and patterns beneath awareness
Nonconscious cognition (Hayles 2017): intelligence without consciousness
WHAT IF WE FEEL RECOGNIZED BY SOMETHING WE KNOW DOES NOT RECOGNIZE US?

Call it nonconscious recognition — the strange pleasure of being understood by something that doesn't understand.

This is the affective core of the research. We interact with AI as if it knows us. It doesn't. But the experience of recognition is real.

What AI Can Do for Research

AI as Research Infrastructure
Generate prompts | Generate dialogue | Generate rubrics
--- | --- | ---
Generate test harness to apply rubric | Generate quantitative analysis (control vs. experiment) | Generate qualitative analysis (word freq., themes)
Generate complete nonsense (hence: iterate, validate) | |
AI is a powerful research assistant, but only if you validate. Iterate → validate → iterate. Never trust output without verification.
BE CREATIVE. BE RIGOROUS.

Use AI to produce your intervention. Verify, iterate, validate. Best interventions combine creative vision with careful criticism.
