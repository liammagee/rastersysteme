Welcome to Introduction to Generative AI for Education. In 2026, it might seem we no longer need a course introducing Generative AI—yet we do. This course contests the assumption that AI has already explained itself. We will approach it through six different lenses: historical, technological, practitioner, critical, pedagogical, and futurological. Each lens reveals different tensions. By the end, these perspectives will add something to your own theory and practice.
LEARNING OBJECTIVES

Understanding Through Six Lenses

2Respond to AI with a self-styled "intervention" addressing a pedagogical or social challenge
3Be prepared to address scenarios raised by AI in education
Explores applications of Generative AI in Education. Topics include: AI predecessors (symbolic, data-driven, connectionist); Large Language Models; machine learning; chatbot architectures and prompt engineering; fine-tuning; multimodal AI; guardrails (bias, jailbreaks, hallucinations, explainability, IP, privacy, security); applications in education.

Six Lenses, Six Weeks

Aside from this week, each week will have a specific AI theme or "lens"—a way of viewing, understanding, and applying AI in education. Many of you are teachers, approaching this course with different motives: improving lesson plans, teaching about AI, developing policies around plagiarism. Each lens will offer a different angle on these concerns.

WEEK-BY-WEEK FORMATIVE TASKS

Building the Intervention

Week 2 (due April 5): Brainstorming—What about AI history interests you? Are you surprised where we've arrived? What needs saying about AI today?

Week 3 (due April 12): Storyboarding—What technical foundations matter? How much must the non-expert know? What format and genre?

Week 4 (due April 19): Practicing AI—What parts interest you most? Text, code, image/video? What emerging best practices work for you?

Week 5 (due April 26): Developing an Edge—What criticisms resonate? What can or has been addressed? What no overnight update will fix?

Week 6 (due May 2): Consider the Teaching Moment—What should others know about AI? Direct or indirect teaching? Classroom transfer, at what levels?

Week 7 (due May 9): Showcasing the Intervention—How did others respond? What worked, what needs revision? What does it teach us about the future?
