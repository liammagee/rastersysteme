Introduction to Generative AI for Education

Welcome to Introduction to Generative AI for Education. In 2026, generative AI is everywhere — and yet there remains something vital about active discussion, about approaching this technology from multiple perspectives rather than narrative consensus. This course will unfold across six lenses: historical, technological, practitioner, critical, pedagogical, and futurological. Each lens reveals different stakes.
ABOUT THIS COURSE

We Approach AI Through Six Lenses

Each perspective complicates the others. By the end, you'll hold these tensions together — not resolved, but understood.

1Historical: where did this come from?
2Technological: how does it work?
3Practitioner: what can we do with it?
4Critical: what are we risking?
5Pedagogical: how do we teach it?
6Futurological: what comes next?
This course was first run last year. We've expanded 90–120 minute sessions into full 2-hour-50-minute workshops: theory + discussion + practice, with a 10-minute break mid-way. No single narrative consensus about AI — instead, six ways of seeing that don't always agree.

THREE STRUCTURAL QUESTIONS

Predicted erasure of white-collar labor. Programming, design, entry-level professions — what happens when AI works properly?
Addictive AI. When AI works, the question isn't capability — it's whether we become compulsive users.
Concentration of power. Who owns AI? Who becomes a power user? What about everyone else?

These are not near-term fixes. They're structural. No prompt engineering or policy tweak resolves them overnight.

What Is This Project?

An experiment conducted over winter to test:

1. Approaches to AI teaching

2. Developments in AI-assisted research

Builds on earlier work: The Drama Machine — Simulating Character Development with LLM Agents

Full paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.10450

This is real research conducted at Illinois. It asks: what happens when we use AI not just to answer questions, but to help us think through complex problems in ways that feel like collaboration?
RECOGNITION & SELF-CONSCIOUSNESS

The central paradox: What happens when we know we're not recognized by a machine but we feel recognized by it?

— Vibe Coding (Karpathy) — intuition before analysis

— Vibe Scholarship — version 1.0 → version 2.0

— Recognition (Hegel, 1807; Honneth, 1996) — central to self-consciousness

— The Unconscious (Freud, 1923) — drives we don't control

— Nonconscious Cognition (Katherine Hayles, 2017) — thinking beyond awareness

What AI Can Do for Research

Generate prompts
Generate simulated dialogue
Generate rubrics for evaluation
Generate test harness to apply rubric
Generate quantitative analysis (control vs. experiment)
Generate qualitative analysis (word frequencies, thematic)
Also: generate complete nonsense — hence, validate constantly
AI is a research assistant that needs supervision. It can accelerate iteration, but every output needs verification. Vibe-then-validate.
