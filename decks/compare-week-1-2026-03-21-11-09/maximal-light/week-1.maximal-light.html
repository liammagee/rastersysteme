Welcome to Introduction to Generative AI for Education!

In 2026, we no longer need a course introducing us to Generative AI for education or for anything else. Yet we remain: because the technology has become "professorial" in ways that demand critical examination. Each week will approach AI through a different lens—historical, technological, practitioner, critical, pedagogical, futurological—to move away from discourses of unbridled hope or hopeless fear. By course end, these perspectives will add to your own theory and practice.

For this week: no readings. We introduce ourselves, discuss the course structure, and consider assessment. The large enrollment signals strong interest despite vast AI materials already existing. This suggests something beyond the technical: a desire for active discussion.

This course expanded from 90–120 minute sessions to 2 hours 50 minutes (with a 10-minute break mid-way) because we frequently ran short on time for discussion and practice. Each session is a workshop: theory, discussion, practice mixed.

Design note: Opening slide held at maximum austerity—near-black field, minimal text, Futura for geometric authority. The palette has nowhere to go but warmer. This is the coldest moment of the deck.

FORMATIVE ASSESSMENT BREAKDOWN

All assessments due Sunday midnight CST. Six weekly tasks × 5% = 30% overall grade. Each response builds toward the final assessment. Aim: 300–500 words per week.

Week 2 (due April 5): Brainstorming an "Intervention" – What has interested you in AI history? Are you surprised at where things have arrived? What needs saying about AI today?

Week 3 (due April 12): Storyboarding – What technical foundations matter? How much does the non-expert need to know? What format & genre are you thinking?

Week 4 (due April 19): Practicing AI – What parts interest you most? Text scaffolding? Code generation? Image/video/music production? What are emerging best practices?

Week 5 (due April 26): Developing an Edge – What AI criticisms resonate? Can they be addressed? What won't an overnight update "fix"? How will you communicate this?

Week 6 (due May 2): Consider the Teaching "Moment" – What should others know about AI? Is your lesson direct or indirect? What could carry to a classroom, and at which levels?

Week 7 (due May 9): Showcasing the Intervention – How did others respond? What worked? What needs work? What does your intervention teach about the future? What did you learn?
