Introduction to Generative AI for Education

Generative AI for Education: Overview

Develop Understanding

Across six thematic lenses: Historical; Technological; Practitioner; Critical; Pedagogical; Futurological
Respond to AI with a self-styled "intervention" addressing pedagogical or social challenge
Address scenarios raised by AI in education
You will develop an understanding of generative AI according to the six themes presented throughout the course. By the end, you will respond with an intervention—a creative, critical artifact—that demonstrates your synthesis of all course perspectives.

Evaluation Criteria

Formative: Cite readings. Demonstrate critical engagement. Appropriate length and tone. Accumulate toward summative.

Summative: Creative responsiveness to challenges. Evidence of critical engagement. Coherence and rigor. Reflection and reflexivity. Theoretical anchoring. Six academic references minimum.

Assessed in total.

AI IN 2026: AN OPINIONATED TAKE

The field has consolidated rapidly. The "Big Three" now dominate, with significant shifts in pricing models and accessibility.

The Big Three Emerge

"Big Three": OpenAI's ChatGPT, Anthropic's Claude, Google's Gemini
Big names have slowed: Xai, Meta, Microsoft Copilot
Apple: using Google Gemini
Amazon: investor in Anthropic, provider for OpenAI
Shift from free / $20/month to advertising and $200/month tiers

Rise of Open Source Models

Mostly developed in China: Xiaomi, Alibaba Qwen, Kimi, Minimax, DeepSeek
Mistral (France)
Nemotron (Nvidia)
Estimated 3–9 months behind commercial models
Issues: benchmarking, IP theft, data exfiltration

Scaling vs. Efficiency

Bigger models, more thinking time, bigger data centers

Scaling laws demand exponential resources

More efficient GPUs (Nvidia)

Increasing demand drives:

But also:

In Week 4 we'll examine criticisms of environmental and social costs. These opposing trends make long-term prediction difficult.

Better algorithms (especially from Chinese labs)

Better hardware (Apple): Open source models run locally

"Bigger Picture" Issues

KEY CONCEPTS

— Vibe coding (Karpathy): intuitive pattern synthesis

— Vibe-then-validate: Version 1.0 → Version 2.0

— Recognition as foundation for self-consciousness (Hegel 1807; Honneth 1996)

— The unconscious (Freud 1923)

— Nonconscious cognition (Katherine Hayles 2017)

— The paradox: What happens when we know we are not recognized but we feel recognized by a machine?

— Nonconscious recognition (Magee 2026)
