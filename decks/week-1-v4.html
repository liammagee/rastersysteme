Welcome to Introduction to Generative AI for Education. In 2026, we might no longer seem to need a course introducing generative AI — for education or anything else. For those following ChatGPT, Claude, or agentic systems like OpenClaw, we may even be reaching a point of singularity in education — where traditional education recedes into the shadows of tradition itself.

This course contests that characterization. Our approach: "generative AI" is an object we need to approach from several perspectives or lenses. Each week outlines one — historical, technological, practitioner, critical, pedagogical, futurological. These do not comprise a single coherent doctrine. I will encourage you to notice and document the tensions between them. By the end, these perspectives should each add something to your own theory and practice.

The course was first run last year with largely the same approach, now expanded to full 2 hour 50 minute sessions. Each session is a workshop — a mix of theory, discussion and practice. For this week there are no readings.
PROVOCATION

Why does this course exist?

In 2026, it can seem difficult to know how to approach a technology that has become so popular precisely because it is "professorial." The large number of students here signals strong interest — as though despite the vast volumes of materials about Generative AI, much generated by generative AI itself, there is still something we wish to get out of active discussion of the topic.

THE OBJECT IS NOT AI. THE OBJECT IS PERSPECTIVE.
Design rationale: rotated title spatializes the conceptual pivot — this is not a technology course but a perspectives course. The rotation forces the audience to reorient.

Aside from this first week, each week has a specific AI theme or "lens." Many of you are teachers undertaking this course with different motives: improving lesson plans with AI, teaching students about AI, developing policies to address AI plagiarism. The lenses address all of these.
OBJECTIVES

Understand. Intervene. Prepare.

Three core objectives distilled: develop understanding of generative AI through the six lenses; respond with a self-styled "intervention" addressing a pedagogical or social challenge; be prepared to address a range of scenarios raised by AI in education.
